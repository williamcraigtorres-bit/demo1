Career Story

Pelé joined Santos as a teenager and made his senior debut at only 15. His speed, balance, intelligence, and astonishing range of finishing made him the centerpiece of a Santos team that became famous around the world.

At 17, he became the youngest player to score in a World Cup final when Brazil won the 1958 tournament. He later added the 1962 and 1970 titles, becoming the only player in history to win three FIFA World Cups.

After nearly two decades with Santos, Pelé joined the New York Cosmos. His arrival transformed soccer's visibility in the United States, and he ended his competitive career by helping the Cosmos win Soccer Bowl 1977.

Why He Ranks #5 All Time

Pelé combined incredible production with unmatched World Cup success. He could score every type of goal, create for teammates, dominate physically, and perform under the greatest pressure.

His influence reached far beyond his trophies and statistics. Pelé helped make football a truly global sport and established the number 10 as the shirt worn by a team's most brilliant attacking player.
